# Phase 1 accepted limitations

These limitations are accepted descriptions of the completed Phase 1 product. They are not automatic Phase 2 commitments.

| Area | Accepted limitation | Consequence | Possible future response | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All national indicators | Aggregates conceal differences among households, workers, firms, industries, demographic groups, and regions. | A national improvement can coexist with worse outcomes for particular groups or places. | Add carefully scoped distributional or regional views after choosing a product objective. | Phase 2 candidate |
| Data timing | Frequencies and publication schedules differ, so the latest periods range from annual 2025 data to weekly July 2026 data in the current snapshot. | Cards should not be read as if every value describes the same date. | Preserve the current latest-period banner; consider release-calendar context later. | Known constraint |
| Source history | Maximum begins at each authoritative source’s first useful observation. Business-investment growth begins in 2008 because the available PNFIC1 source begins in 2007; household debt service and HOAM begin in 2005. | Long-run comparisons are not equally available for every topic. | Use a different authoritative source only if it improves the measure without reconstruction or conceptual drift. | Known constraint |
| Revisions and vintages | Committed files contain the latest provider history, not the values available to decision-makers at earlier dates. GDP, payroll, national-account, and other observations can be revised. | The dashboard cannot evaluate forecast accuracy or historical decisions using real-time information sets. | Store vintages or use a real-time database if policy or forecast evaluation becomes a goal. | Phase 2 candidate |
| Refresh operations | Refresh is manual and requires a developer FRED key; there is no scheduler or runtime backend. | Deployed values remain static until a validated refresh is committed and released. | Automate the existing validated workflow with explicit monitoring and review. | Phase 2 candidate |
| Missing source observations | Missing source values remain chart gaps. Derived CPI measures can have a longer gap when an exact 3- or 12-month endpoint is unavailable. | Some periods cannot support a valid derived rate, and lines may be discontinuous. | Keep gaps visible; add annotations only if they remain factual and maintainable. | Known constraint |
| Canvas charts | ECharts renders the primary visualization to canvas, whose internal marks are less accessible than native HTML. | Screen-reader and keyboard users rely on labels, factual summaries, native range and zoom controls, and semantic tables rather than individual canvas points. | Continue improving noncanvas equivalents; evaluate another renderer only with evidence. | Known constraint |
| Forecasting and events | Phase 1 has no forecasts, recession shading, policy-event annotations, or automated turning-point claims. | It supports historical description but not explicit forward-looking or event-study analysis. | Add these only after defining whether the next objective is investment, forecasting, or policy analysis. | Phase 2 candidate |
| Broad credit conditions | The Chicago Fed NFCI credit subindex replaces the originally contemplated corporate credit spread. It is a standardized composite, not a directly observed spread. | It covers broader credit conditions but cannot answer questions about one bond market or borrower class. | Revisit licensed or narrower spreads only if their added value justifies redistribution and history constraints. | Approved substitute |
| Bank lending standards | DRTSCILM is a quarterly survey net percentage for domestic-bank C&I standards covering large and middle-market firms only. The sample, questions, and reporting convention may evolve. | It is not a census, denial rate, loan-volume measure, demand measure, or evidence about households, small firms, mortgages, commercial real estate, or every bank. | Add another SLOOS category only if a future objective justifies the narrower scope and added complexity. | Known constraint |
| Corporate profit share | CPATAX divided by nominal GDP is a revised national-accounts aggregate that includes corporations beyond publicly traded U.S. companies. | It is not a company revenue margin, stock-index earnings measure, valuation, expected earnings, earnings per share, household-welfare measure, or complete labor-versus-capital split; it conceals industry and company differences. | Add market or distributional profit measures only under an explicitly narrower objective, and preserve vintage data if historical decision analysis is required. | Known constraint |
| Housing affordability | Atlanta Fed HOAM is a national model for a median-income household purchasing a median-priced home. | It does not describe local markets, current owners, or every buyer profile. | Add regional or buyer-specific analysis only under a distributional or housing-focused objective. | Known constraint |
| Wages | `CES0500000003` is average hourly earnings for all private nonfarm employees, not a median; it excludes benefits, government workers, and the self-employed, and its compatible year-over-year real-growth history begins in 2007. | The wage card does not represent every worker, total compensation, or isolate workforce-composition effects. Rounded public FRED index levels can also differ by 0.1 percentage point from BLS's published real-earnings calculation. | Consider complementary median, distributional, or total-compensation measures. | Phase 2 candidate |
| Payroll growth | The prominent measure is an absolute three-month average monthly job change. | It is intuitive but less comparable across distant decades as total employment grows. | Evaluate normalized payroll growth as a companion, not an automatic replacement. | Phase 2 candidate |
| Initial unemployment claims | Weekly claims can be revised and temporarily distorted by filing behavior, administrative disruptions, weather, strikes, disasters, unusual events, and seasonal adjustment. They also exclude laid-off workers who do not file or qualify. | A short-lived spike or decline is not by itself proof of a labor-market turning point or recession. | Keep the official four-week average prominent and interpret claims alongside unemployment, payroll growth, and prime-age employment. | Known constraint |
| Effective tariff burden | Customs receipts divided by goods imports is an average effective burden, not a statutory schedule or estimate of who bears the economic cost. | It cannot describe product- or country-specific rates, nontariff barriers, or incidence. | Add statutory or incidence analysis only with a narrower trade-policy objective. | Known constraint |
| Bundle size | The lazy shared ECharts chunk exceeds Vite’s default 500 kB advisory threshold. | Chart code downloads only when needed but remains a relatively large deferred dependency. | Reassess chart-library scope if measured loading performance becomes a problem. | Known constraint |

## Approved Phase 1 substitutions and scope decisions

- Broad credit stress uses `NFCICREDIT` instead of a corporate credit spread because it offers long, redistributable, nonproprietary history and complements the separate rate card. Its composite nature is disclosed.
- Industrial activity uses capacity utilization alongside manufacturing output rather than adding a second broad industrial-production card. It contributes operating-intensity information without duplicating manufacturing output.
- The epic’s optional additional labor participation or leading measure was deferred during Phase 1. Story 22 subsequently added initial unemployment claims as a timely complementary labor measure.
- Housing affordability uses the official Atlanta Fed HOAM workbook because FRED does not supply the approved modeled ownership-cost series.
